**Cascade, 2026-09-20 (lead).** Seams v3 made the budget one integer of money per objective, removed the standing budget and everything that served it, and named the effect classes `read`, `propose`, `act`. In this plan: `Budget` has one field; the standing node, its lock, `allocate_turn`, `standing_remaining`, `raise_standing`, the turn-parented carve, and `TestStandingConservation` are gone; `raise_budget` on the objective and an objective-less Scribe as its own root take their place; `delegate` consumes no `descendants` and nothing consumes `cards` or `tool_calls`; class ceilings are names. The Findings, Seam amendments, and Critique sections below are the record of earlier rounds and still speak of the old shape where they quote it.

## Purpose

M0 proves one objective end to end at `propose` inside one client space's sandbox, in code and in a drafted message, and that a stop leaves durable state intact and the sandbox's compute dead (tech stack §10). The tree is where that objective exists: the node with its contract and its committed budget in dollars, the approval that binds a revision, the Brief that carries an attenuated capability set, a seat, a sandbox profile, and a generation to one worker, the ledger that conserves money under concurrency, the generation fence that makes a stop bite, and the projection that rebuilds all of it from the event store after a kill. The two first objectives are each one leaf, so at M0 the tree is a set of roots, each with its Executor, Verifier, and Scribe Briefs, plus the occasional Scribe of a turn that opened no objective, which is a root of its own. Without this component nothing has a budget to spend, no worker has a token to present, and nothing can be stopped.

### Ledger arithmetic

Remaining is always derived, never stored (spike 01). Two kinds of node hold a budget of their own: an objective, whose `B` is its current contract's budget plus `Σ grant` over its rows (each grant a person's raise), and a root Scribe, whose `B` is its one `grant` row. A Brief carved from an objective holds what was allocated to it: its `B` is its one `allocate` row. Every write is one row of a fixed shape:

| Write | `node_id` | `brief_id` | `kind` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `_allocate(node, child, amount)`: a Brief from an objective | the node | the Brief id | allocate |
| `raise_budget(objective, by, approval_id)` | the objective | null; `approval_id` set | grant |
| `delegate` of an objective-less Scribe | the Scribe's brief id | null | grant (the kernel-set amount) |
| `consume` on a Brief | `parent(x)`, or `x` for a root Scribe | `x` | consume |
| `release(x)` | `parent(x)` | `x` | release |

For any id `x`, over `budget_ledger`:

- `parent(x)` is the `node_id` of the `allocate` row naming `x` as `brief_id`; an objective and a root Scribe have none
- `allocated_live(n) = Σ allocate − Σ release` over rows with `node_id = n`
- `consumed(x) = Σ consume` over rows with `brief_id = x`
- `remaining(n) = B(n) − allocated_live(n)` for an objective; `remaining(x) = B(x) − consumed(x) − Σ release(x)` for a Brief, clamped at zero
- `Objective.budget_allocated = allocated_live(objective)`; `Objective.budget_consumed = Σ consume` over every row with `node_id = objective`, since a Brief's `consume` row carries `node_id = parent(x)`

Conservation (seams §1.3): `allocated_live(n) ≤ B(n)` for every node and `consumed(x) ≤ B(x)` for every Brief, with one stated exception: an `incurred` consume may take `consumed(x)` past `B(x)` by the amount `budget.overrun` records for `x`, after which `remaining(x)` is zero and no reservation succeeds (README, "still answers and says so, and the overrun is an incident"). Every write that could break the rule takes `lock_key` of the node it charges, reads remaining, and inserts only if the amount fits. Nothing else spends: the ledger sees money at delegation and at the gateway, and no tool call, card, or child count touches it (seams v3, item 1).

- `_allocate(conn, node_id, child_id, amount)`: under `lock_key(node_id)`, `amount.fits_within(remaining(node))` or `BudgetExceeded`; one `allocate` row.
- `raise_budget(conn, objective_id, *, by, approval_id)`: under the objective's lock; refuses an `approval_id` whose record is not of kind `approved` (`approval_check`), writes one `grant` row for `by` with `approval_id` set. The record's card kind is the supervisor's to assert, since the tree reads no card; the row cites the approval so the raise is a lookup (architecture §4, "only a person raises the root").
- `consume(conn, x, amount, *, incurred=False)`: `x` is a Brief id. Under `lock_key(parent(x) or x)`. `incurred=False` refuses past zero with `BudgetExceeded` before writing. `incurred=True` records the full amount; when it would go below zero it appends `budget.overrun` with `amount` and `shortfall` and returns `ZERO`. No generation check, because the gateway records a cut call's reserved `max_tokens` after the stop that cut it (tech stack §4). At M0 the gateway is the only caller; `incurred=False` exists for the M1 Planner's child reservations.
- `release(conn, x)`: for a Brief with a parent, writes one `release` row for `remaining(x)` under `parent(x)` and returns it; a second call finds zero, writes nothing, returns `ZERO`. For a root Scribe it writes nothing and returns `ZERO`, since there is no parent to return to. Refused for an objective.

### Reading the node

`project(conn, objective_id)` reads the `objectives` row (`id`, `parent_id`, `depth`, `space_id`, `conversation_id`), then `events.read_for(conn, space_id=..., key="objective_id", value=objective_id)`, the ledger rows with `node_id = objective_id`, and the child ids, and hands all four to `fold(row, events, ledger_rows, children)`. `fold` is a pure function of those inputs so the chaos test replays it from the same row and events (integration plan, task 7) and so the properties below run it thousands of times without a database. The fold: `id`, `parent_id`, `depth`, `space`, `conversation_id` from the row; contract and revision from the last `objective.contract_revised` or `objective.opened`; `approved_revision` from the last `objective.approved` if its revision equals the current revision, else `None`; state and reason from the last `objective.state_changed`, else `FRAMED`/`framed`; `generation = 1 + count(brief.stopped)`; `owner_brief` = the last Executor `brief.issued` that has no later `brief.stopped`, `brief.failed`, or `report.landed`; `reports` from `report.landed` with the event id and the report's summary; `evidence` concatenated from those reports; `budget_allocated` and `budget_consumed` from the ledger rows as above.

### Opening, revising, approving

`open_objective` calls `check_may_open(space, spaces)` first (it raises for `unassigned` or an unknown space), then refuses, before any row is written: a `ceilings.max_effect_class` above the space's `max_effect_class` (architecture §9); a deadline not in the future; a `contract.root` absent from `Space.roots` (seams §1.4). It mints a UUIDv7, inserts `objectives` (depth 0, no parent) and `objective_revisions` (revision 1), appends `objective.opened`, and returns the id. Decided here: no state event is written at open; the fold reads `FRAMED`/`framed` as the state of a node with no `objective.state_changed`.

`revise_contract` is allowed only in `FRAMED` and `AWAITING_APPROVAL` (seams §3.2; the framing and commit loop, including a reply with edits) and refused with `IllegalTransition` in every other state. Decided here, from architecture §2: work after Commit runs against the approved revision, and a change during work is scope drift, which returns the node to `AWAITING_APPROVAL` first. It refuses a budget below `allocated_live + own_consumed`, a ceiling above the space's, and a root outside the space. It inserts the revision row, appends `objective.contract_revised`, and returns the revision. Clearing the approval is a fact of the fold, not a write.

`approve(conn, objective_id, approval)` calls `approval_check(approval, contract_revision=current)` (it raises `NotAnApproval` for a kind that is not `approved`, `approved_with_edit`, or `self_approved`, and `StaleRevision`), then refuses a record whose `objective_id` differs and a `self_approved` record on a contract with `max_effect_class == "act"` (architecture §2). From `FRAMED` or `AWAITING_APPROVAL` it appends `objective.approved` then `objective.state_changed` to `APPROVED`/`approved`. From `FAILED` it does the same only when the record's kind is `approved` for the current revision, which is the `retry` reply to a `verification_failure` card or the `grant` reply to a `budget_increase` card after `raise_budget` (seams v3 §1.12; architecture §8, revival is an escalation); a `self_approved` record on a `FAILED` node is refused, so the model never revives its own failure. The node keeps its reports and its generation. The tree gates nothing on the size of the budget: the estimate is the supervisor's and there is no ceiling on it (seams v3 §1.12, Architect 2026-09-20).

### The state machine

Decided here: architecture §2 names the states and the typed reasons; it does not tabulate the edges. This table does, using only the `StateReason` values of seams §1.4, and seams §3.2 cites it. `transition(conn, objective_id, state, reason)` refuses any pair not in it with `IllegalTransition`, refuses a reason outside the pair's set, and refuses the pairs marked "only" for every caller but the named function. Cancel reasons `C = {deadline, card_expired, stopped_by_person, subtree_revoked, space_ended}`.

| From | To | Reasons | Written by |
|---|---|---|---|
| FRAMED | AWAITING_APPROVAL | commit | supervisor (always for an `act`; by its judgment at `read` and `propose`, seams v3 §1.12) |
| FRAMED | APPROVED | approved | `approve` only |
| AWAITING_APPROVAL | APPROVED | approved | `approve` only |
| FAILED | APPROVED | approved | `approve` only, on the `retry` reply or the `grant` reply after `raise_budget` (seams v3) |
| APPROVED | RUNNING | running | `delegate` of an Executor only |
| APPROVED, RUNNING | AWAITING_APPROVAL | challenged_assumption, effect_class_elevation, awaiting_decision | `land_report`, supervisor (`budget_increase` stays in the `StateReason` literal for the M1 Planner and is written by nothing at M0; overrun is `FAILED/budget_exhausted`) |
| RUNNING | VERIFYING | verifying | `land_report` only |
| RUNNING | FAILED | worker_failed, budget_exhausted | `land_report`, supervisor |
| VERIFYING | SUCCEEDED | verified, verification_sampled_out | `land_report`; `verify_objective` for `verification_sampled_out` |
| VERIFYING | FAILED | verification_failed, worker_failed, budget_exhausted | `land_report` for `verification_failed`; `verify_objective` for a kernel verdict, a Verifier run that failed or aborted, and a node that cannot fund the Verifier |
| VERIFYING | AWAITING_APPROVAL | awaiting_decision | `land_report` (abstain) |
| any of the six live states | CANCELLED | C | supervisor, `approvals.expire_due` (`card_expired`), `tree.expire_due` (`deadline`) |
| SUCCEEDED, CANCELLED | none | | terminal |
| FAILED | APPROVED only, as above | | otherwise terminal |
| PAUSED | not entered or left at M0 | | out of scope |

A transition into `CANCELLED`, `FAILED`, or `AWAITING_APPROVAL` calls `stop` on the node inside the same transaction (seams §3.2), because architecture §2 revokes and cancels the subtree in one store transaction and architecture §8 wants no process running past its node. `SUCCEEDED` and `VERIFYING` stop nothing: the reporting Brief is already terminal and a Scribe may still be writing the record. Every transition appends `objective.state_changed` with `from`, `to`, `reason`.

### Class rules

Decided here, from the sections cited in each row: the per-class facts the kernel enforces whatever the prompt says (architecture §6). `delegate` refuses a request outside its row.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Executor | read, write, bash, ask, push_branch, connector.read | the node's (architecture §3.1) | worktree, scratch (tech stack §6) | PROJECT (architecture §3.1) | frontier (tech stack §4.1) | APPROVED, RUNNING |
| Verifier | read, bash | `read` (tech stack §6, `verify`) | verify | PROJECT | verifier | VERIFYING |

| Planner | refused at M0 | | | | | tech stack §10 |

The Verifier has no `ask` (seams §1.5, ruling 10): a question to the supervisor is a channel back to the Executor's account, and blindness is a fact about what the Verifier can reach (architecture §5). One Executor per leaf: a second Executor request while one Brief is live is refused with `AlreadyOwned` (architecture §3.2). A Scribe may attach to a node in any state, including a terminal one, or to no node at all when the turn has no open objective (seams §1.5), because the standing paperwork is written after the turn (architecture §3.5); its budget is carved from the turn's objective when there is one and is its own kernel-set root when there is none (architecture §4; seams v3 item 5).

### Delegate, step by step

Under `lock_key(objective_id)` when there is an objective, and under `lock_key(brief_id)` for a root Scribe, in one transaction:

1. `project` the node when there is one. Refuse if the state is outside the class's row, if a stopped Brief on the node has no `brief.stop_confirmed` (`Unconfirmed`; tech stack §4, replacement only after the old compute is confirmed stopped), if the deadline has passed, or if an Executor is live and another is requested. Without an objective: refuse any class but Scribe and any `parent_brief` but a turn id.
2. Node capability set: `ceiling(root_capabilities(space), contract.ceilings.max_effect_class)`, or `ceiling(root_capabilities(space), "propose")` for an objective-less Scribe. Effective issuer: the node's set when `parent_brief` is `None` or a turn id (the supervisor delegating; seams §3.2), else `issuer` (a worker's Brief capabilities; refused at M0 by `kernel/api.py`, seams §2.4). Then `issue(effective, request.capabilities)`, which refuses rather than clips; then the class row's names and class ceiling; then every scope's `space_of` equals the space.
3. Ceilings: `Ceilings(max_effect_class=contract ceiling, deadline=contract deadline, max_data_class=request.max_data_class)`; refuse `request.max_data_class` above the class row or above the contract's. Decided here: an objective-less Scribe gets `max_effect_class="propose"` and `deadline` = one hour from issue, since a Scribe writes the turn's paperwork and exits (architecture §3.5) and a deadline is the one time bound (architecture §4).
4. Context slice: `slice.space` equals the space; `slice.sha256 == ContextSlice.digest(slice.blocks)`; every block's `data_class` at or below `max_data_class` (seams §1.5, "the kernel refuses a Brief with a block above it").
5. Budget. With an objective: `_allocate(conn, node_id=objective_id, child_id=brief_id, amount=request.budget)`, whatever `parent_brief` is (a turn id only says the supervisor asked); `BudgetExceeded` when it does not fit. Without one (a root Scribe): one `grant` row under `node_id = brief_id` for `request.budget`, which the supervisor sized from the fork at the summarizer's price (seams v3 item 5). Nothing else is consumed: there is no `descendants` axis (architecture §4, decomposition is bounded by money and the leaf criterion).
6. Seat: `infra.models.load().model(seat).id` by the class row. Generation: the node's current, or 1 without a node. Brief id: `new_id()`.
7. Sandbox profile: `profile_for(request.sandbox_profile, space, key, artifact_kind=contract.artifact_kind, max_data_class=request.max_data_class, snapshot=request.snapshot, root=contract.root)` with `key` the objective id for `worktree` and the brief id otherwise (seams §2.2 with Round two). An objective-less Scribe passes `artifact_kind="document"` and `root=None`, so `profile_for` falls back to `choose_root`. Token: `await token_issuer.issue_token(conn, brief_id=..., generation=..., model_ref=..., space=...)`.
8. Writes: `briefs` row (Brief minus token, plus sha256), the ledger rows from step 5, event `brief.issued`, and, for an Executor on an `APPROVED` node, `objective.state_changed` to `RUNNING`/`running`.
9. Return the Brief with the token. The token is never stored and never appears in any event (seams §1.5).

### Landing a terminal

`land_report(conn, token, terminal)` under the node's lock. `check_generation(token)` first; the one exception is `terminal.outcome == "aborted"` on a Brief the node has already stopped, which returns without writing, because the stop already closed it and `runs.stop` writes the terminal row itself (seams §3.5). Then, by the Brief's class and the terminal:

- Executor, `report`: append `report.landed`; transition `RUNNING` to `VERIFYING`/`verifying`. Before that, for every `AssumptionDelta` whose status is `challenged` or `refuted`: refuse if no contract assumption has that statement; else append `assumption.challenged` with the node as `ancestor_id` (at M0 the node is the only holder on its path to root; the lookup walks `parent_id` and stops at the first contract holding the statement), transition to `AWAITING_APPROVAL`/`challenged_assumption`, which stops the node's other live Briefs, and skip the `VERIFYING` transition. Deltas of status `open` or `supported` are recorded in the report only.
- Verifier, `report` holding a `Verdict`: append `report.landed`; `pass` transitions to `SUCCEEDED`/`verified`, `fail` to `FAILED`/`verification_failed`, `abstain` to `AWAITING_APPROVAL`/`awaiting_decision`. This is the one writer of the verdict transition (seams §3.2, ruling 1); `verify.record_verdict` records and never transitions.
- Scribe, `report`: append `report.landed`; no transition. An objective-less Scribe's `report.landed` carries `objective_id: null`.
- Any class, `failed`: append `brief.failed`; an Executor's failure transitions `RUNNING` to `FAILED`/`worker_failed`; a Verifier's or Scribe's failure changes no state here (`verify_objective` transitions a failed Verifier run itself, and `brief.failed` is a supervisor trigger, seams §3.3).

The `Report`'s artifact hashes against the tool log are checked by `kernel/runs.py` before the terminal reaches the tree (seams §1.6, §3.5). Releasing the Brief's unspent allocation is the caller's separate `release` call, so a stop and a report share one code path for it.

### Stopping

`stop(conn, objective_id, reason, *, token_issuer=None)` under the node's lock calls `stop_brief` for every live Brief (issued, no `report.landed`, `brief.failed`, or `brief.stopped`) and returns the ids; the caller passes them to `kernel.runs.stop` outside the transaction (seams §3.2, §3.5, ruling 4), which aborts the worker, stops the sandbox with a probe-confirmed receipt, calls `confirm_stop`, writes the terminal row, and calls `release`. `stop_brief(conn, brief_id, reason, *, token_issuer=None)` appends `brief.stopped` with `new_generation` = the generation after this event and awaits `token_issuer.revoke(conn, brief_id)`; the supervisor calls it directly for an objective-less Scribe (seams, Round two). The state is the caller's to change, except where `transition` called `stop` itself. `confirm_stop(conn, brief_id, receipt)` appends `brief.stop_confirmed` with the `StopReceipt`. Until every stopped Brief on a node is confirmed, `delegate` refuses on that node, which is the sentence from tech stack §4 made into data. `check_generation(conn, token)` raises `StaleGeneration` when the Brief is unknown or `token.generation < 1 + count(brief.stopped)` for its node; a Brief with no node has generation 1 and is stale only after its own `brief.stopped`. A turn token names no Brief and carries no generation; the gateway never calls `check_generation` for it and revocation alone ends its calls (seams, Round two), so the tree issues no generation for a turn and `stop_brief` refuses a turn id.

`expire_due(conn) -> list[ObjectiveId]`: for every live node whose contract deadline has passed, transition to `CANCELLED`/`deadline`, which stops its Briefs (seams §3.2; architecture §2 and §8). `supervisor.serve` calls it once a minute beside `approvals.expire_due`, which writes the `expired` record and transitions nothing; a card's expiry coincides because the card carries the deadline (seams, Round two).

### The two paths that matter

The code change at `propose`, end to end: `open_objective` (FRAMED, with the supervisor's estimate and basis on the contract) → `approvals.self_approve` then `approve` (APPROVED) → `delegate(Executor, worktree)` (RUNNING; the gateway calls `remaining` before each call and `consume(..., incurred=True)` after, in money) → terminal → `land_report` (VERIFYING) → `release` → `runs.run_brief` stops, confirms, snapshots → `verify.verify_objective` sizes the Verifier from `remaining(objective_id)` → `delegate(Verifier, verify, snapshot=ref)` → verdict → `land_report` (SUCCEEDED or FAILED) → `release`. On FAILED, the `verification_failure` card's `retry` reply mints an `approved` record and `approve` returns the node to APPROVED with its reports kept. On overrun the gateway's `consume` records the shortfall, the Executor's next call is refused for `budget`, its run ends `failed`, `land_report` writes `FAILED/budget_exhausted`, and the `budget_increase` card's `grant` reply goes `raise_budget`, `approve`, and a fresh Executor delegation. The drafted reply is the same path with `artifact_kind = message`, the inbound item in `Contract.inputs`, and no `push_branch`. Every turn along the way touches the ledger only through the implicit Scribe: delegated with `parent_brief=turn_id`, carved from the turn's objective, or its own root when the turn opened none. A turn's own model phase is recorded in the gateway log against its cap and never in the ledger (seams v3 item 2).

Stop: `stop` (fence and revoke, one transaction) → `runs.stop` (abort, `SandboxProvider.stop`, `confirm_stop`, terminal row, `release`). Every later call carrying the old token fails `check_generation`; the gateway refuses it as `stale_generation` or `revoked`; the next `delegate` on the node carries the new generation.

## Properties

Everything here is under `kernel/` or `schemas/`, so each invariant is a Hypothesis property with the operations it ranges over, in the pattern of spike 01 (stateful, against the real database) and spike 05 (pure, 2,000 examples).

`tests/test_capability_properties.py`, pure, 2,000 examples each, over sets of up to six `Capability` values drawn from every `CapabilityName`, the three classes, and scopes from two space ids with up to two path segments:

`tests/test_tree_properties.py`, against the local database with the fakes bound, one root per example:

Product Requirements Draft for review August 2026

Quiet Participant

A meeting agent that listens to the whole call and writes in the chat only when it can answer something the room actually asked.

Summary

Invite it to the meeting like a colleague. It joins, listens, and stays silent — until someone asks a question it can answer, at which point it posts the answer in the meeting chat while the question still matters.

This is not a notetaker. Notetakers optimize what you can recall afterward. This optimizes what you can decide during. The two products share an input and almost nothing else.

Problem

Meetings run on unanswered questions

Every working meeting generates a handful of questions that nobody in the room can answer from memory: what did we decide last time, is that ticket still open, what was the actual number, did the customer ever reply. The information almost always exists somewhere the participants have access to.

But retrieving it costs more than asking is worth. Someone would have to stop listening, find it, and interrupt to deliver it. So the group does one of three things instead: guesses and moves on, defers the decision by a week, or assigns "I'll check after" to someone who often doesn't.

The gap is not knowledge. It is that no participant is both fully present and free to go look things up.

Goals

What success looks like

  • Answers land while they still matter. A question raised at 14:02 is answered in the chat before the topic closes — not in a summary afterward.
  • Every comment earns its place. Participants should be glad it spoke, every time. A single unnecessary comment does more damage than three missed opportunities.
  • It is invited, not installed. Whoever runs a meeting decides whether it attends, using the same mechanism they use for people. No admin rollout, no per-meeting configuration.
  • It leaves a usable trace. What it heard and what it said are retrievable afterward and feed whatever memory the wider system keeps.

Explicitly not goals

  • Transcription, minutes, or summaries as a product. That market is well served; competing there wastes the interesting part.
  • Speaking aloud. Text only. Voice raises the cost of every mistake and solves nothing this product needs.
  • Facilitation — timekeeping, agenda enforcement, prompting quiet participants.
  • Taking consequential action mid-meeting. It answers; it does not send, deploy, book, or commit on anyone's behalf.
  • Any observation about the people in the room: performance, tone, participation, sentiment.
Principles

The product is a judgment system

Capturing audio and generating text are solved problems available to any competent team. The entire product risk sits in one decision, made continuously: is this a moment to speak? These principles exist to keep that decision from drifting toward chattiness, which is the natural failure direction.

Silence is the resting state
Not speaking is the correct behavior for the overwhelming majority of a meeting. The system does not need a reason to stay quiet; it needs a reason to speak.
Precision over recall
Missing an answerable question is a small loss, invisible to the room. Posting something unwanted is a visible cost paid by everyone present, and it compounds — a few of those and people stop reading the chat.
Answer, never narrate
"Looking into that" and "good question" are noise wearing the costume of helpfulness. If there is no answer yet, there is nothing to post.
Cite or stay quiet
Every claim carries where it came from — a document, a ticket, a prior meeting, a record. An answer nobody can check is a guess with better formatting, and in a room making decisions that is worse than silence.
Late is a different message
An answer that arrives after the conversation has moved on is not the same contribution delivered slowly — it is an interruption. Either frame it explicitly as a callback to the earlier point, or drop it.
Scenarios

Three moments it exists for

Engineering sync · minute 6
"Wasn't there a bug filed about this exact thing? I feel like we already looked at it."
Posts: the two matching open issues with titles, ages, and current status, linked. The room reads them and drops one line of planned work.
Customer call · minute 22
"You mentioned a deadline last time — was it the end of the quarter or the end of the month?"
Posts: the quoted line from the previous call's record, with its date. Nobody has to promise to check and follow up.
Planning review · minute 41
"I think we're at about sixty percent on that migration, roughly."
Posts: the current figure from the tracked source, dated. The estimate was low by a third, and the plan changes in the meeting rather than the week after.

The pattern in all three: something checkable was said or asked, the answer existed, and the cost of retrieving it was the only reason it went unretrieved.

Capabilities

What it has to be able to do

Stated as capabilities, not components — any sufficiently capable agent system should be able to satisfy these with its own architecture.

A

Accept an invitation

It holds an identity that can be invited to a meeting the way a person is. The invitation is simultaneously the trigger to attend, the permission to be there, and the record of who granted it. Withdrawing the invitation is the complete off switch, and needs no other mechanism.

B

Arrive prepared

Before the meeting starts it reads whatever the invitation exposes — subject, description, participants, attached material — and pulls related context from the systems it can reach. Preparation is the single largest lever on comment quality. A cold arrival produces generic remarks and generic remarks are the failure mode.

C

Follow the conversation

It maintains a running, speaker-attributed record of what is being said, accurate enough to tell a question from an aside and to know who asked. It does not need perfect transcription; it needs reliable turn boundaries and enough fidelity to catch names, numbers, and references.

D

Judge whether to speak

Continuously and cheaply, it evaluates the recent window for a speakable moment: an open question, a checkable claim, a named artifact whose state it knows. It defaults to no. It applies a hard budget — a ceiling per hour and a cooldown after each comment — so that even a correct judgment cannot produce a flood.

E

Answer at two speeds

Moments answerable from what it already holds are handled immediately. Moments needing real retrieval or reasoning are dispatched to deeper work that runs without blocking its attention on the meeting. It must never stop listening in order to think.

F

Write for a meeting chat

Short, self-contained, scannable in the two seconds someone spends glancing away from a speaker. It states the answer first and the source second, and it never posts something that requires leaving the meeting to understand.

G

Check that the answer is still wanted

Between deciding to answer and posting, the conversation keeps moving. Immediately before publishing, it re-checks its answer against the current state of the discussion and posts, reframes as an explicit callback, or discards. This is the requirement most easily forgotten and the one that separates a helpful participant from a laggy one.

H

Hand off what it learned

After the meeting, what was discussed and what it contributed are retained where the wider system can use them — so the next meeting starts better prepared than this one did.

Measurement

How we know it is working

Conventional engagement metrics actively mislead here. More comments is not better; time-in-meeting is not better. The metric set is built around precision, with volume as a guardrail rather than a goal.

MeasureDefinitionTarget
Comment usefulness Share of posted comments rated worth reading by an attendee, collected as one lightweight prompt after the meeting ≥ 70%
Volume ceiling Comments posted per hour of meeting — a guardrail, never a target to grow ≤ 4
Time to answer Median seconds from the triggering moment to the comment appearing < 20s
Staleness rate Share of comments landing after the room has moved on, judged from the record < 10%
Answerable-miss rate Questions that went unanswered in the meeting but that it could have answered — measurable only while running silently Track, don't optimize
Kill criterion

If usefulness cannot be held above 70% across a month of real meetings, the product does not work and no amount of retrieval quality rescues it. The judgment is the product; ship it when the judgment is good, not when the plumbing is.

Constraints

What to verify before committing to a platform

Meeting platforms differ sharply in what they permit, and the difference decides whether this product is buildable there at all. Establish both halves before design work starts:

  • Can it attend? Some platforms offer an official interface for accessing live meeting audio; others do not, and every available route is some form of joining as an ordinary participant.
  • Can it post? Separately and more restrictively — official interfaces frequently allow listening while offering no way to write into the meeting chat. Listen-only access reduces this product to a notetaker.

Where a platform allows one but not the other, the honest options are to route the output somewhere else the participants already are, or to attend as an ordinary participant and accept the maintenance and account risk that comes with it. Decide this deliberately; it is not a detail that can be deferred to implementation.

Standing constraints regardless of platform

  • Visible presence. It appears in the participant list under a name that makes clear what it is. A listening agent should never be a surprise to anyone in the room.
  • One meeting at a time, at least initially. Overlapping invitations resolve to the first and log the skip.
  • Access is inherited, never expanded. It can retrieve only what the meeting's participants could already retrieve themselves.
Risks

What will go wrong

RiskWhy it happensResponse
Chattiness creep Every tuning pass that fixes a miss loosens the gate; nothing pushes back the other way Hard volume ceiling in code, and usefulness reviewed on a fixed cadence rather than on complaint
Confident wrong answers Retrieval finds something plausible and adjacent rather than correct Mandatory sourcing; no source means no post, enforced as a rule not a preference
Chilling effect People speak differently knowing a system is listening and may respond Per-meeting invitation, visible presence, and an obvious way to remove it mid-meeting
Sensitive material Meetings drift into compensation, personnel, legal matters Recognize the drift and fall fully silent; retain nothing from those stretches
Platform fragility The attendance route depends on a surface the vendor can change without notice Isolate everything platform-specific behind one boundary; expect to repair it repeatedly
Rollout

Earn the right to speak

The sequence matters: each phase exists to validate the judgment before more people are exposed to it being wrong. Do not compress it.

01

Silent

It attends and posts nothing. Every comment it would have made is written to a log with the moment that triggered it. Read the log, not the meeting.

Exit when a reviewer reading the log agrees with the great majority of the calls, including the decisions to stay silent.
02

Assisted

Proposed comments go privately to one participant who can forward them into the chat with a single action. The human veto is the safety net and, more usefully, the label.

Exit when the forward rate is high enough that the veto is no longer doing meaningful work.
03

Live

It posts directly, starting with meetings whose participants opted in knowingly. Widen only as the usefulness measure holds.

Reverse when usefulness drops below target for two consecutive weeks — return to assisted rather than tuning in place.
Open

Unresolved

  • Should it answer when addressed directly by name, bypassing the relevance gate entirely? Convenient, and the most likely route to the chattiness it is designed to avoid.
  • Does it correct a factual error nobody asked about? High value when right, and the fastest way to become unwelcome when wrong.
  • Where does the usefulness rating actually come from — a prompt after every meeting will be ignored within two weeks.
  • What does it do in a meeting whose participants did not know it was coming, when the person who invited it never mentioned it?

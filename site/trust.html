07 · trust and safety

It stops when told, keeps a record it cannot edit, and acts only within each task's ceiling.

You can let it into your chats and repositories knowing that a stop halts it at once and that every action and model call it pays for is a row you can read. It works under its own accounts, never yours.

01 · What it cannot do on its own

Every effect passes one broker, inside a ceiling

The kernel, Valor's deterministic core, holds its credentials, and every effect made with them passes one broker. Each effect has a class: read; propose, reversible, such as a branch; or act, irreversible, such as a merge, a send or a payment. Every task has a ceiling, a child task's never above its parent's, and the broker refuses anything above it. A merge leaves only when the tests and a fresh review have passed.

Inside the ceiling nothing waits for a tap from Tom, who built Valor and runs Yudame; every act that leaves is reported to him. Valor acts as itself on its own accounts. It never acts as Tom, and holds none of his accounts.

Every model call goes through a gateway that prices it on its task in the ledger. Spending is metered and shown, and nothing caps it.

One known gap: a turn runs as the Mac's user, so an account reached through the keychain or a logged-in session acts outside the broker. Tom accepted this on 2026-10-01. Known limits

Source: core/broker.py · docs/architecture.md, Effect classes · docs/mission.md, Constraints · persona/identity.toml · core/spending.py

02 · Stop and correction

A stop and a correction are each one ledger row

A stop writes one row to the ledger, the append-only record of everything Valor does. The gateway and the broker read it before every model call and effect, so the task and every task under it stop at once. The turn's processes are killed and every call is charged, though an effect already sent can't be called back. A test stops a task mid-turn and checks that nothing is left open.

A correction from Tom is a numbered row carrying who gave it, how and when. Every turn renders the corrections in force as it starts, so one given now reaches tasks already running. Tom withdraws or replaces one with a later row; the original stays in the ledger and no turn renders it again.

Source: docs/architecture.md, Stop · core/corrections.py · tests/test_serve.py

03 · What leaves the Macs

What still leaves Valor's Macs

Valor's record stays on its machines, but some of the work doesn't:

  • Each model call goes to the model provider, Anthropic by default, through the kernel's gateway, which records it.
  • Classification calls, such as judging whether a request is clear enough to build, go to Jev, a hosted model, with an open-weight model on OpenRouter as the fallback.
  • Messages pass through Telegram, Slack and email, and code goes to GitHub.

So the model provider sees each prompt it answers. The record of the work stays on Valor's Macs: who asked for what, what was done, what it cost and what Tom corrected.

Source: docs/tech-stack.md · docs/architecture.md

04 · Governance

No new limit without a written grant

A new check, gate or denial must name the mission item it serves and an incident that already happened without it, and needs Tom's grant in the plan file that adds it. A bug fix never adds one.

Each guard in force is ledgered with its incident, its mission item and a ninety-day expiry; one that never fired is listed for Tom and deleted unless he keeps it. The full text is on Rules.

Comparisons: Valor and other AI coworker agents

Several products now offer an agent that works on its own for one person or a team. This page compares Valor with the closest of them on the two things Valor is built around, independence and data sovereignty, and links to a page for each of the five closest. Facts about other products were checked on 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22, and link to their sources.

What the terms mean

Independence and data sovereignty

Independence
Valor runs on its own Macs, one per client, and acts as itself on its own accounts: its own email and Google Workspace seat, its own GitHub and chat accounts. It never acts as Tom and holds none of his accounts.
Data sovereignty
Valor's record of its work stays on those Macs: the ledger of every call, turn and effect, the turn transcripts and the memory store, all in Postgres on the machine. The code is public under the GPL.

Four of the products below run the agent on the vendor's computers, and by default all six act under your accounts. OpenClaw can use a phone number of its own on WhatsApp, and Meta has announced an email address of Muse's own.

What leaves Valor's Macs

What still leaves Valor's Macs

Valor's record stays on its machines, but some of the work doesn't:

  • Each model call goes to the model provider, Anthropic by default, through the kernel's gateway, which records it.
  • Classification calls, such as judging whether a request is clear enough to build, go to Jev, a hosted model, with an open-weight model on OpenRouter as the fallback.
  • Messages pass through Telegram, Slack and email, and code goes to GitHub.

So the model provider sees each prompt it answers. The record of the work stays on Valor's Macs: who asked for what, what was done, what it cost and what Tom corrected.

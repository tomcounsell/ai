Product and projects

Valor is a product owner and a project manager at Yudame as well as an engineer. He keeps each product's backlog, decides when an item is ready to build, keeps the decisions open for Tom in one place per project, and handles research, invoices and email drafts.

Product rooms

A backlog Sheet for each product

A product room is a chat where Valor acts as the product owner. Each room has a Google Sheet that Valor keeps:

  • A goal on top, with its outcome metric. The people who own the product set them; Valor proposes and asks.
  • Two lanes: mandatory work and bets. Value is high, medium or low, and appetite is a cap of one, three or six weeks. There are no scores.
  • A rank for ready items only. The rest wait in order of how far they are shaped.
  • Blockers listed apart, each with an owner and a date to chase. Valor chases once, then takes it to Tom.
  • A review after each ship, 14 days on by default: keep, iterate or revert, with the reason.

Readiness

Valor approves what goes to build

People give the input: the goal, the appetite, the job to be done, and the answers only they know. Valor runs the readiness review and sends what passes to engineering himself (Tom, 2026-11-01).

The review has twelve checks, including these:

  • the item serves the goal and stays inside its job;
  • the problem has observed or reported evidence, not only an assumption;
  • the solution fits the appetite and names code that exists;
  • acceptance is written as statements a reviewer can test;
  • each question an engineer would ask is answered, and a question only a person can answer is put to them while the item waits.

An item that passes is posted in the project's dev chat, where engineering starts it.

Decisions

A decision board per project

Each project's Slack channel can hold a canvas listing the decisions open for Tom on that project, with Valor's recommendation on each, and those decided recently. Clients never share a board.

Tom answers in the chat, with a reaction, or on a call; he does no work on the board itself. The ledger holds each question and answer, and the canvas is rebuilt from it when they change.

Running the work

From a message to a report

In each chat, Valor's session reads what came before and decides what a message needs: an answer, or a task. A task carries the work, asks its questions in one batch with a stated default for each, and reports back to the session, which tells the chat in plain words.

A team chat is Tom and the people he works with, and any of them can ask Valor for work. Valor answers the person who asked, and only what only Tom holds goes to Tom.

Calls

Google Meet calls

Asked for a call in a group chat, Valor reads up on the topic, posts a Meet link in the thread and joins as himself. In the call he answers when addressed: aloud to what was said, in the call's chat to what was typed. A call about a project can go through that project's open decisions. When the call ends, its summary goes to the thread it was asked in.

Research

Reports and podcasts

A topic for research goes to a deep-research run: several research services, claims checked against each other, and a long report with sources and a confidence on each claim, returned as a PDF. A run takes 25 to 45 minutes.

On Tom's word, the report becomes an episode on the public Yudame Research podcast, or on Yudame Staff for private topics. Publishing never holds up the report.

Operations

Invoices and email

Invoices

Valor creates Yudame's client invoices in the company's Mercury account, each with a pay link, once Tom has approved it, and sends its notice from valor@yuda.me to the client. He lists who hasn't paid when Tom asks. He sends no payment reminders.

Email drafts

Asked for a draft, Valor writes it in the asker's voice and hands it back as text for them to send. A send to anyone outside the chat goes only when the asker says send, or on a standing say-so from Tom.
